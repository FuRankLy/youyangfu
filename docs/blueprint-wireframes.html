The idea

Chinese characters are called 方块字, square block characters. You learned them inside the squares of 田字格 paper. Everything on this site is built from one material: small squares on one shared lattice, assembling into whatever each chapter needs.

  • One materialEverything that moves is made of squares on the same lattice: your name, the map, the chart, the avatar. Text stays real text, crisp and selectable.
  • A quiet page with three loud momentsThe opening, the flight to Auckland and the life chart. Everything between them is still, typographic and precise.
  • The visitor drives the motionScrolling plays the scenes and the cursor plays the details. Only the opening and live data (clocks, the current candle) move on their own.
  • Two scripts as equalsChinese appears where it carries meaning: your name, places, chapter numerals. Never as decoration.
  • Fast and kindEvery scene has a still version for slow phones and for people who turn motion off. Nothing important hides behind an animation.

Chapters are numbered in 大写 financial numerals, 壹 贰 叁 肆 伍 陆. They are the forms written on cheques and invoices so an amount can't be altered, which suits a site about someone whose surname, 付, means "to pay", and whose life is drawn as a market chart.

On every page

The frame around the story. It stays small and out of the way, and every piece of it uses the same squares.

Header
Your seal links home. Work, About, Play, Contact and Resume sit on the right, with the current chapter underlined in cinnabar. It hides as you scroll down and comes back when you scroll up. On phones it becomes a menu button that opens a full-screen list.
Colour mode
One small square that cycles Light, Dark and Now. Now is the default and follows the real sun over Auckland. It's set before the first paint, so the page never flashes the wrong colours.
Hold'em progress
Visitors are dealt two face-down cards at the opening. The flop turns over at The chart, the turn at Stack, the river at Off the clock, and the showdown happens at Let's connect. It doubles as the page's progress indicator.
Page transition
Squares sweep out from where you clicked, the page changes underneath, then they clear. About half a second.
Cursor and sound
The normal cursor. Squares near it grow slightly, as in the prototype. Sound plays only when you ask for it, like tapping your name's pinyin.

Opening

Phase 1
What you see
The prototype: a faint 米字格 grid, 付优扬 writing itself in 17 strokes, the squares flying into Frank Fu, the seal stamping. Then one plain line about who you are.
What moves
About 6 seconds the first time, and scrolling skips it. Repeat visits get a 1.5-second version. Hover the name to switch scripts. Later, hovering a character shows its parts (person, hand, rising sun), and tapping the pinyin plays your recording.
The intro line
"Software engineering student at the University of Auckland. Born in Zhengzhou, raised in Auckland." It names no year, so it never goes stale. Wherever your year of study does appear (resume, chart), the site works it out from your 2024 start, and it switches to graduate wording after 2027.
壹

Two homes

Phase 1 layout, phase 2 scene
What you see
A map made of squares. Zhengzhou lights up and its 米-shaped high-speed rail lines draw outward. The view pulls back, an arc flies 10,163 km to Auckland while the clock jumps forward, and then the split of your life appears.
What moves
Scrolling scrubs the flight. "Flip the world" mirrors the map across the equator, and Zhengzhou at 34.7°N lands almost exactly on Auckland's latitude, 36.8°S. The percentage counts up live: right now it reads 50.0% of your life in Aotearoa, assuming you arrived in mid-July.
Content needed
A short paragraph on growing up between the two. The exact arrival day if you can find it.
贰

The chart

Phase 1 static, phase 2 scene
What you see
Your life as a candlestick chart, one candle per chapter. The Zhengzhou years trade on the Zhengzhou Commodity Exchange: prices in ¥, red means up. July 2016 opens a gap and the chart lists on NZX: prices in $, green means up.
How to read a candle
Open and close are how the chapter started and ended. The top of the wick is its best moment and the bottom is its hardest. Flags under the axis mark events: born, moved, started university, the crossover.
What moves
Candles draw one by one as you scroll, then the view zooms into the Auckland years, where school, university and internships sit. The crosshair snaps between candles, axis numbers roll like an odometer, and the current candle is still forming. A ticker tape runs along the top.
Also
A List switch shows the same chapters as plain text, for skimming and for screen readers.
Content needed
Your chapters with dates, and a line each for the best and hardest moment. You decide how personal those get.

Let's connect

Phase 1, working
What you see
A short form: name, email, a topic chip that becomes the subject line, and a message. Your links sit beside it, with a copy button for your email.
What moves
On send, the message folds into a square, your seal stamps it, and the button reads "Sent". If something fails, it says what went wrong and keeps the message. The note under the button uses Auckland's current time to say when you'll likely reply.
Under the hood
A server action checks the fields and sends the email to you through Resend. Your address never appears in the page, and a hidden decoy field plus rate limiting keep spam out.
Content needed
The address the messages should go to, and your links.

Design system

The shared rules every section is built from. Changing a token here changes it everywhere.

Colour

Paper and ink carry almost everything. Cinnabar, the colour of seal paste, is the only accent: the seal, the wet tip of a stroke, and "up" in the Zhengzhou years. Jade appears only in the chart. Now mode blends between the two sets by the sun over Auckland.

Dark Paper #13161B Ink #E7E9E4 Soft #8C929B Grid #2C313A Line #454C57 Cinnabar #E0563D Jade #4FB08B

Chart colours

The colour for "up" flips at the move, the same way the convention flips between Chinese and Western markets.

Type

Instrument Sans for all Latin text, set tight at large sizes. LXGW WenKai for Chinese: a Kai face that matches the brush strokes of the opening, loaded only for the characters the site uses. Numbers use tabular figures wherever they line up.

Layout

12 columns with 24px gutters (16px on phones) and a 1440px maximum width. Every size snaps to an 8px lattice, the same lattice the squares sit on. Running text is left-aligned and stays under about 68 characters. The name and the set pieces are centred.

Motion

Four easing curves and a handful of durations, used everywhere. Consistent timing is what makes motion feel expensive. Scroll scenes don't have a duration: your scrolling drives them.

BrushWriting strokes.55, 0, .35, 1
FlightSquares leaving and landing, 720ms.7, 0, .2, 1
SettleInterface responses, 320ms.2, .8, .2, 1
OutReveals, 600ms.16, 1, .3, 1

Hover feedback takes 160ms. Anything the visitor touches answers within 100ms. With reduced motion turned on, scenes become still frames and transitions become quick fades.

Build plan

Phase 1 builds the whole frame and style with every chapter in place, so content and set pieces can be added later without restructuring anything.

Phases

  1. Foundation

    • Design tokens, fonts, Light / Dark / Now with no flash on load
    • Header, phone menu, footer with live clocks, smooth scrolling, square-wipe transitions
    • Squares engine and the opening, ported from the prototype, with the short repeat-visit version
    • All six chapters at their real layout, reading placeholder data from content files; a static chart with hover
    • Contact form working end to end
    • Case study template with one sample, resume page, plain 404
    • Search and sharing: titles with all three names, a share image, seal favicon, sitemap
    • Deployed to Vercel with a preview link for every change

  2. Set pieces

    The Two homes scroll scene, the full chart scene with ticker and live candle, the Hold'em progress corner, character breakdowns, your name's audio.

  3. Real content

    Projects and case studies, school and internships on the chart, the Stack data, the resume PDF.

  4. Off the clock

    The cube-built avatar with gear hotspots, the smash meter, the Table and the Arcade.

  5. Delight and polish

    Hidden cards around the site, the 404 rally game, sound, a performance pass, analytics.

Quality bar

  • Phone Lighthouse scores: performance 90 or more, accessibility 100
  • Useful first view within 2.5 seconds on 4G; the opening plays while the rest loads
  • 60 frames per second on a mid-range laptop; the squares engine uses fewer squares on weaker devices
  • Everything reachable by keyboard and screen reader; reduced motion gives still frames
  • No layout shift and no flash of the wrong colours
